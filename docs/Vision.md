# TripFlow AI — Vision

## 1. Our Vision

### Not a Travel Planner. A Travel Companion.

TripFlow AI is not designed to create a perfect itinerary before a trip.

It is designed to understand the traveler, the trip, and the situation in real time — and continuously help the traveler decide what to do next.

Our long-term vision is to build a:

> **Personal Travel Agent — a Travel Jarvis that travels with you.**

TripFlow should become an AI that understands the context of a trip and adapts as the trip unfolds.

---

## 2. The Problem

Most travel planning tools assume that travelers know exactly what they want to do.

The typical workflow looks like this:

1. Choose a destination.
2. Find places to visit.
3. Create a daily itinerary.
4. Optimize routes.
5. Follow the itinerary.

But real travel rarely works this way.

Plans change constantly.

- You wake up later than expected.
- A restaurant has a long wait.
- The weather changes.
- You spend more time somewhere than planned.
- Your children get tired.
- A place turns out to be more interesting than expected.
- You discover somewhere new.
- You simply change your mind.

At that point, the carefully created itinerary becomes less useful.

The real problem is not:

> "How do I create the perfect itinerary?"

The real problem is:

> **"Given where I am, what I want, and what is happening right now, what should I do next?"**

TripFlow is built around this question.

---

## 3. A New Travel Experience

### Before the Trip

TripFlow does not require travelers to plan every hour.

Users can simply provide:

- Destination
- Accommodation
- Places they want to visit
- Restaurants or shops they are interested in
- Travel companions
- Transportation preferences
- General travel preferences

TripFlow uses this information to understand the overall context of the trip.

The goal is to reduce planning effort, not increase it.

---

### During the Trip

Once the trip begins, TripFlow continuously evaluates the current situation.

It can consider:

- Current location
- Current time
- Weather
- Opening hours
- Waiting times
- Transportation options
- Travel distance
- Remaining time
- Previous decisions
- Travel companions
- User preferences

Instead of simply showing a predefined itinerary, TripFlow recommends the most appropriate next action.

For example:

> "You have about two hours before dinner. The weather is good, and three places you saved are nearby. The museum is currently less crowded than usual. Would you like to go there?"

The system is not trying to control the trip.

It is helping the traveler make better decisions with less effort.

---

## 4. Designed for Flexible Travelers

TripFlow is especially designed for travelers who do not want to plan every detail.

These travelers may know:

> "We're staying here, and we definitely want to visit these places."

But they do not necessarily want to decide:

> "At 10:15 we will leave the hotel, spend 47 minutes here, then take this route..."

TripFlow embraces this style of travel.

### Less Planning

Reduce the amount of work required before the trip.

### More Freedom

Allow travelers to change their minds without breaking the entire itinerary.

### More Discovery

Help travelers discover places they did not originally plan to visit.

### Continuous Adaptation

Let the plan change naturally as the trip changes.

---

## 5. Location-Agnostic by Design

TripFlow is not a Jeju travel app.

Jeju may be a useful early use case, but the product should work across different destinations and transportation systems.

For example:

- Jeju — rental car
- New York — subway, walking, taxi
- Paris — Metro and walking
- Tokyo — rail and walking
- European cities — trains and public transportation
- Road trips — driving
- Mixed transportation — walking + transit + taxi

TripFlow should understand the transportation context of each destination.

The goal is not to optimize for one city.

The goal is to build a **general travel intelligence layer**.

7. TripFlow as a Continuous AI Agent

TripFlow should not behave like a chatbot that simply answers questions.

It should behave like an agent.

The agent should:

Understand the traveler's intent.
Understand the current travel context.
Gather information from relevant tools.
Evaluate possible actions.
Recommend the best next action.
Learn from the traveler's decision.
Re-evaluate when the situation changes.

This creates a continuous loop:

Observe
   ↓
Understand
   ↓
Reason
   ↓
Act / Recommend
   ↓
Observe Again
   ↺

This is the foundation of the Travel Jarvis concept.
